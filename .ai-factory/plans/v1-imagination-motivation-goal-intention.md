## Roadmap Linkage
Milestone: "M3 — Cognition and Providers"
Rationale: This feature replaces the remaining V1 cognition placeholders with subjective future simulation, independent drives, goal-aware deliberation, and executable intention selection.

## Goal

Implement a deterministic V1 cognition policy that imagines several structured futures exclusively from the agent's observation and owner-scoped subjective state, keeps conflicting drives independently inspectable, manages active goals, evaluates subjective risk including opportunity-based fear of death, selects one contextual intention, and translates it into exactly one fresh closed `AgentCommand` for normal `WorldEngine` admission.

## Design Decisions

- Keep imagination strictly subjective. `ImaginationEngine` may consume the owned observation-derived situation, physiology projection, active goals, `SelfModel`, directed relationships, semantic beliefs, and reconstructed memories; it must never receive `WorldState`, `ObservationBatch`, objective event repositories, replay state, hidden physical rules, or actual world probabilities.
- Treat belief confidence, remembered experience, future confidence, and subjective outcome probability as distinct values. Conflicting or inaccurate beliefs are valid inputs and may cause two agents in the same objective situation to imagine different risks and choose different actions.
- Represent each `ImaginedFuture` as one actionable direction with typed target data, expected effects on independent drives and goals, typed risks, expected directed social effects, confidence, uncertainty, horizon, and source references. Keep candidates bounded, ordered, uniquely identified, and safe to inspect without retaining hidden rationale.
- Model these drives independently at minimum: hunger, thirst, safety, fatigue, belonging, curiosity, status, autonomy, competence, predictability, and novelty. A drive has a stable disposition/baseline and a contextual activation; neither is a personality class or a global reward.
- Extend goals with a structured, cognition-readable desired outcome and progress/lifecycle metadata while preserving owner scope, priority, and human-readable description. Only active goals enter deliberation; goal effects remain separate from drive effects.
- Do not persist or optimize a single permanent reward score. Intention selection uses a versioned deterministic multi-criteria policy over candidate vectors: reject infeasible candidates, apply contextual safety/critical-need vetoes, remove Pareto-dominated options, compare pairwise support across currently activated drives and active goals, account for relationships/self-model/uncertainty, then use documented stable tie-breaks. Boundary artifacts retain the separate evidence and effects used by the policy.
- Model fear of death as anticipated opportunity foreclosure, not a hard-coded `death_penalty`. For each candidate, combine the agent's subjective mortality estimate with currently outstanding goal value, attachment/dependency effects, safety activation, autonomy loss, and reduction of future option space. An agent with different goals, attachments, beliefs, memories, or perceived alternatives can therefore avoid or accept the same objective risk differently.
- Keep terminal truth authoritative: `AgentRuntime` still stops cognition after an observed dead self. Anticipatory mortality appraisal belongs only to cognition and cannot declare an agent dead or alter physical rules.
- Replan from the latest observation each tick and submit only one command. The selected future/intention is non-authoritative; the planner must construct a fresh exact member of the existing `AgentCommand` union, with `Wait` as a safe fallback when no candidate is feasible or sufficiently supported.
- Preserve deterministic execution through explicit policy versions, finite bounded numbers, quantization where arithmetic is aggregated, canonical input ordering, stable IDs, and explicit tie-breaks. Do not use global RNG, wall-clock defaults, UUIDs, Python `hash()`, or free-form narrative parsing.
- Keep logs metadata-only. Never log observation payloads, goals/descriptions, belief claims/values, reconstructed memory content, relationship dimensions, imagined effects, risk values tied to semantic payloads, action text, or hidden reasoning.
